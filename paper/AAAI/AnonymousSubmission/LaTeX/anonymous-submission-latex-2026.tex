\begin{abstract}
Open-weight checkpoints are routinely fine-tuned, quantized, pruned, merged, and redistributed without reliable lineage metadata. Existing provenance tools are brittle: hashes fail after any weight change, metadata requires trust, and watermarks must be inserted before release. \textbf{We study a post-hoc white-box weight-lineage verification: given two checkpoints with compatible residual architecture, do they share weight-level lineage after common checkpoint transformations?} This is distinct from behavioral similarity: a distilled student imitates its teacher's outputs, but its weights are trained from scratch and share no lineage.

We show that residual-network training induces an intrinsic fingerprint: correctly paired residual-branch products develop a diagonal-dominant fingerprint, measured by a trace/Frobenius score, that is absent at initialization and in non-residual controls. We use this signal to align blocks between checkpoints and compute a model-level lineage score from centered branch signatures. Across 12 architectures spanning language, vision, and speech (GPT-2 to LLaMA-2, ViT, Whisper, ResNets), the fingerprint recovers correct projection pairings with 80--100\% accuracy, and the lineage score achieves complete separation: fine-tuned, pruned, quantized, and LoRA-merged checkpoints score high, while independently trained models and distilled students remain low. Unlike activation-based CKA and IPGuard, which track functional similarity, this provides a passive, retroactive provenance signal for white-box residual checkpoints.
\end{abstract}

The open-weight ecosystem has transformed how neural networks are distributed and reused. When Meta released LLaMA~\citep{touvron2023llama}, the community produced instruction-tuned variants within weeks. Platforms like Hugging Face now host hundreds of thousands of model checkpoints~\citep{huggingface2024}, many of which are fine-tuned~\citep{devlin2019bert,ouyang2022rlhf}, quantized~\citep{han2016deep,dettmers2022llmint8}, pruned~\citep{han2015deep}, or merged~\citep{wortsman2022model,ilharco2023editing}. This creates a \emph{model supply chain} where downstream checkpoints inherit weights from upstream models, but the lineage is often undocumented or intentionally obscured. Provenance of AI-generated content is now treated as a first-class problem: Google's SynthID watermarks text and images at generation time~\citep{dathathri2024synthid}, yet no analogous mechanism exists for the models themselves. Model reuse is easy; reliable model provenance is not.

Existing provenance mechanisms fall short (Table~\ref{tab:comparison}). Cryptographic hashes break after any weight modification. Metadata and model cards~\citep{mitchell2019model} require honest distributors and preserved records; they can be falsified or lost. Watermarks~\citep{uchida2017embedding,adi2018turning} must be inserted before release; they cannot be applied retroactively to already-deployed checkpoints, and no surveyed scheme proved robust against fine-tuning or pruning~\citep{lukas2022sok}. Behavioral tests that compare model outputs cannot distinguish weight inheritance from imitation: two models can behave similarly without sharing weights, especially after distillation~\citep{hinton2015distilling}. Activation-based similarity measures like CKA~\citep{kornblith2019similarity} and decision-boundary fingerprints like IPGuard~\citep{cao2021ipguard} inherit this limitation. We need a passive, post-hoc signal that reads provenance from the weights themselves.

We ask: given a reference checkpoint and a suspect checkpoint with the same residual architecture (e.g., both LLaMA-2-7B derivatives), can we determine whether the suspect inherited weights from the reference, even after common transformations like fine-tuning, quantization, or pruning? This is distinct from duplicate detection (which hashes solve until the first modification), behavioral similarity (which conflates imitation with inheritance), and watermark verification (which requires forethought). The question is whether shared weight ancestry leaves detectable structure after modification.

The answer is yes. We find that training itself leaves a characteristic fingerprint in the weights of residual networks. In these architectures~\citep{he2016deep}, each block adds a learned transformation to its input via a skip connection (Figure~\ref{fig:hero}a). During training, the input and output projections of each block align so that their product $W_{\mathrm{out}}W_{\mathrm{in}}$ develops diagonal-dominant structure that is absent at random initialization and does not appear in networks without skip connections (Figure~\ref{fig:hero}b). This structure emerges from the optimization process: it is learned, not an initialization artifact (Section~\ref{sec:diagonal-dominance}).

We exploit this fingerprint for provenance verification. Each residual block yields a signature: its branch product scored for diagonal concentration via a trace/Frobenius ratio (Equation~\ref{eq:dd-score}). Correctly paired blocks from the same trained model score high, while mismatched or unrelated blocks do not. The extraction is architecture-aware, respecting the specific structure of Transformer MLPs, attention paths, and ResNet bottleneck blocks (Table~\ref{tab:factorization}). To compare two checkpoints, we align their blocks using these signatures, aggregate the pairwise similarities into a model-level lineage score, and threshold it against a null distribution built from unrelated models (Equation~\ref{eq:lineage-score}).

The fingerprint is not specific to one model family. We observe it in public checkpoints across language, vision, and speech (GPT-2, BERT, LLaMA-2, Mistral, Qwen2.5, DeepSeek-R1, ViT, Whisper, and ResNets) where it recovers block correspondences with over 91\% accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:transformer-family}). Turning to the verification task itself, we construct controlled lineage benchmarks on MLPs and ResNet-18: models with genuine weight ancestry (produced by fine-tuning, weight noise, pruning, quantization, or LoRA merging) are cleanly separated from those without (independent training runs and distilled students), with AUROC = 1.000. The distillation case is instructive: a student can closely replicate its teacher's outputs, yet our score correctly labels it unrelated, because its weights carry no trace of the teacher's training (Figure~\ref{fig:hero}c). Finally, on the public LLaMA-2 family, the method flags three real fine-tuned descendants (chat, Vicuna, CodeLlama) as related while rejecting four independently trained checkpoints, among them OpenLLaMA, which shares the architecture but inherits no weights.

Our contributions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Diagonal-dominant fingerprint.} We establish that gradient-based training drives the input and output projections of residual blocks toward mutual alignment, concentrating their product along the diagonal, a property absent at random initialization and never acquired by skip-free architectures.
\item \textbf{White-box verification method.} We convert this signature into a practical test: per-block scores are matched across two checkpoints, aggregated into a single lineage statistic, and judged against a null distribution, requiring no watermark, no metadata, and no cooperation from the model's distributor.
\item \textbf{Broad empirical validation.} We evaluate on 246 checkpoint pairs covering 12 architectures and 5 model families, from synthetic benchmarks with known ground truth to real-world derivatives of LLaMA-2, obtaining 80--100\% block correspondence and AUROC$=$1.000.
\item \textbf{Robustness and specificity.} The fingerprint persists through fine-tuning, quantization, pruning, and LoRA merging, yet vanishes for distilled students and independently trained models of identical architecture, a distinction beyond the reach of behavioral measures such as CKA and IPGuard.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{White-Box Model Lineage Verification}

A verifier receives two checkpoints $A$ and $B$ and determines whether they share weight-level lineage. We assume white-box access and compatible residual architectures (same $L$ and $d$); cross-architecture comparisons are out of scope. The verifier operates on weights alone: no training data, activations, or forward passes.

Checkpoints are \emph{related} if they share a common weight ancestor through transformations $\theta' = T(\theta, D)$: fine-tuning, RLHF, pruning, quantization, or LoRA merging. They are \emph{unrelated} if initialized independently, even when architecture, data, task, or outputs match. Linear merges induce partial lineage (Appendix~\ref{app:harder-bench}). The verifier returns \textsc{Related}, \textsc{Unrelated}, or \textsc{Inconclusive}. The score is symmetric, $\mathcal{L}(A,B) = \mathcal{L}(B,A)$; directional attribution requires metadata (Appendix~\ref{app:verification}). Block-level correspondences are recovered as a diagnostic (Section~\ref{sec:pairing}).

Lineage follows initialization, not behavior. A distilled student trained from fresh weights is unrelated to its teacher, regardless of output fidelity; model-extraction copies via query access~\citep{tramer2016stealing} are likewise unrelated. Conversely, a student initialized from teacher weights is related regardless of the training objective. The method is a forensic primitive for weight-level provenance, complementing cryptographic signing and watermarking where those were never applied.

\subsection{Related Work}

Table~\ref{tab:comparison} summarizes the comparison.

Cryptographic hashes break after any weight modification. Model cards~\citep{mitchell2019model} require honest distributors and can be falsified when checkpoints are redistributed. Proof-of-learning~\citep{jia2021proofoflearning} requires retained training transcripts that already-released checkpoints lack.

Parameter watermarks~\citep{uchida2017embedding} and backdoor watermarks~\citep{adi2018turning} embed deliberate signals during training. \citet{lukas2022sok} found no surveyed scheme that reliably survived fine-tuning and pruning, and all require proactive insertion: unwatermarked legacy checkpoints cannot be verified.

IPGuard~\citep{cao2021ipguard} extracts inputs near classification boundaries; adversarial frontier stitching~\citep{lemerrer2020adversarial} marks decision surfaces with adversarial examples. CKA~\citep{kornblith2019similarity} and SVCCA~\citep{raghu2017svcca} compare representations on a probe dataset. All four measure functional similarity and cannot distinguish inheritance from imitation: a distilled student may share decision surfaces while having independent weights. They also require data and forward passes.

Weight cosine, Frobenius distance, and permutation-aligned matching~\citep{ainsworth2023git} detect derived checkpoints when the suspect remains close in parameter space, but these are uncalibrated global scalars with no structural account of lineage. HuRef~\citep{zeng2025huref} relies on LLM parameter convergence after pretraining. REEF~\citep{zhang2024reef} and neural lineage detection~\citep{yu2024neurallineage} need probe data or activations. MoTHer~\citep{horwitz2025mother} recovers model trees but requires a checkpoint collection rather than a single pair. Our fingerprint is data-free, operates on a single pair against a calibrated null, and localizes inheritance to specific blocks.


\section{Training-Induced Diagonal Dominance in Residual Branch Products}\label{sec:diagonal-dominance}

A residual block computes $x_{\ell+1} = x_\ell + F_\ell(x_\ell)$, where $F_\ell$ is the residual branch. For a branch with $K$ linear layers $W_1, \ldots, W_K$ interleaved with nonlinearities, the \emph{residual branch product} composes the linear factors, dropping nonlinearities:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:branch-product}
M_\ell = W_K W_{K-1} \cdots W_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}
\end{equation}
Individual factors $W_k$ are rectangular and do not share an input--output basis; their composed product maps the residual stream back into its own coordinate space, so its diagonal entries compare like with like. The exact factorization is architecture-dependent (Table~\ref{tab:factorization}).

\subsection{Why Residual Training Induces Diagonal Dominance}\label{sec:mechanism}

Under the Frobenius inner product $\langle A, B \rangle = \mathrm{tr}(A^\top B)$, the identity matrix and the traceless matrices span orthogonal subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$. Any branch product therefore splits into a component along the identity, with coefficient $\langle M_\ell, I \rangle / \langle I, I \rangle = \mathrm{tr}(M_\ell)/d$, and an orthogonal traceless remainder:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomp}
M_\ell = -\varepsilon_\ell I + E_\ell
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_\ell = -\mathrm{tr}(M_\ell)/d$ and $\mathrm{tr}(E_\ell) = 0$.
Traces are predominantly negative (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}), so $\varepsilon_\ell$ is typically positive; the score uses magnitude.

The \emph{diagonal-dominance score} measures this concentration:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dd-score}
s(M) = \frac{|\mathrm{tr}(M)|}{\|M\|_F},
\end{equation}
the fraction of the matrix's energy along the identity direction, up to $\sqrt{d}$. Training moves energy into the identity component: correctly paired branch products score far above unpaired or untrained ones (Figure~\ref{fig:hero}b).

The skip connection places the learned branch in an identity-centered parameterization: training optimizes a correction around an explicit identity path. For stable gradient flow, the block Jacobian $J = I + J_F$ should remain near-orthogonal (dynamical isometry~\citep{pennington2017resurrecting}). If $J \approx I$, then $J_F \approx 0$; for small learned corrections, the linear component of $J_F$ tends toward $-\varepsilon I$. Because $W_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $W_{\mathrm{out}}$ receive coupled gradients through the branch, such pressure aligns their product with coordinate-preserving directions. Non-residual networks lack this constraint: without the skip connection, nothing couples $W_{\mathrm{in}}$ with $W_{\mathrm{out}}$ more than with any other weight matrix.

No signal at initialization under any scheme: seven initializations show chance-level pairing before training, including orthogonal initialization, which satisfies dynamical isometry yet has learned nothing~\citep{saxe2014exact} (Appendix~\ref{app:architectures}). No signal without the skip connection: PlainNet trained to comparable loss develops none (Table~\ref{tab:plainnet-control}).

\subsection{Margin Analysis}\label{sec:margin}

By orthogonality of decomposition~\eqref{eq:decomp}, $\|M_\ell\|_F^2 = \varepsilon_\ell^2 d + \|E_\ell\|_F^2$: the score depends on $M$ only through the ratio of identity to traceless components:

\begin{proposition}[Correct-pair score]\label{prop:margin}
Let $M = -\varepsilon I + E$ with $\mathrm{tr}(E) = 0$ and $\varepsilon \neq 0$. Then:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:margin-formula}
s(M) = \frac{\sqrt{d}}{\sqrt{1 + \|E\|_F^2 / (\varepsilon^2 d)}} \leq \sqrt{d}
\end{equation}
with equality if and only if $E = 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$|\mathrm{tr}(M)| = |\varepsilon| d$ and $\|M\|_F^2 = \varepsilon^2 d + \|E\|_F^2$ by orthogonality. Substitute into (3).
\end{proof}

Geometrically, $s(M)/\sqrt{d}$ is the cosine of the angle between $M$ and the identity. Mismatched products have no such concentration: $W_{\mathrm{out}}$ and $W_{\mathrm{in}}$ from different blocks have no preferred alignment, giving

\begin{corollary}[Mismatched baseline]\label{cor:baseline}
For products of projections from independent blocks, $\mathbb{E}[s] \approx 1/\sqrt{d}$.
\end{corollary}

The margin scales as $d$: wider networks separate correct from incorrect pairings more cleanly. Empirically the correct-pair score grows as $d^{0.87}$ (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}), consistent with $\|E\|_F/(\varepsilon\sqrt{d})$ shrinking as $d$ grows while remaining below the $\sqrt{d}$ bound.

\section{From Diagonal-Dominance Fingerprints to Lineage Verification}\label{sec:verification}

Diagonal dominance certifies trained residual structure, not ancestry: unrelated trained models both exhibit it. The decomposition~\eqref{eq:decomp} separates the two signals. The identity component is generic across trained models. The traceless component $E_\ell$ is checkpoint-specific: it survives weight-preserving transformations but not reinitialization. Verification runs on $E_\ell$, with diagonal dominance gating which branches are trustworthy.

\subsection{Centered Residual Signatures and Block Alignment}\label{sec:pairing}

For each branch product $M_\ell$ we retain only the traceless remainder of decomposition~\eqref{eq:decomp}, normalized to a unit vector:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centered-fingerprint}
R_\ell = M_\ell - \tfrac{\mathrm{tr}(M_\ell)}{d} \cdot I, \quad
\phi_\ell = \tfrac{\mathrm{vec}(R_\ell)}{\|\mathrm{vec}(R_\ell)\|_2}
\end{equation}
$R_\ell = E_\ell$ from~\eqref{eq:decomp}. The signature $\phi_\ell$ carries checkpoint-specific geometry that weight-preserving transformations inherit and independent training cannot reproduce. Because $M_\ell$ is invariant under hidden permutation and reciprocal rescaling, so is every signature built on it (Section~\ref{sec:boundaries}).

Given reference $A$ and suspect $B$ with $L$ blocks each, we form the similarity matrix $G_{ij} = \langle \phi^A_i, \phi^B_j \rangle$, gated by diagonal dominance (Appendix~\ref{app:verification}), and recover the block correspondence via the Hungarian algorithm~\citep{kuhn1955hungarian}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hungarian}
\pi^* = \arg\max_{\pi \in \mathcal{P}_L} \sum_{i} G_{i,\pi(i)}
\end{equation}
For derived checkpoints preserving block order, $\pi^*$ should be the identity; recovering it without metadata confirms that signatures track block identity. The correspondence matrix also shows which blocks were inherited (Appendix~\ref{app:architectures}). Complexity: branch products $O(Ld^2 h)$, similarity matrix $O(L^2 d^2)$, assignment $O(L^3)$.

\subsection{Lineage Score and Verification Test}\label{sec:lineage-score}

The lineage score averages signature similarity over aligned branches:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lineage-score}
\mathcal{L}(A, B) = \frac{1}{L} \sum_{\ell=1}^{L} \langle \phi^A_\ell, \phi^B_{\pi^*(\ell)} \rangle
\end{equation}
Each term is a cosine, so $\mathcal{L} \in [-1,1]$ with $\mathcal{L}(A,A) = 1$. The score is symmetric (Appendix~\ref{app:verification}). Related checkpoints score near 1; unrelated ones near 0, since independently initialized weights produce uncorrelated $E_\ell$.

We calibrate against a null distribution $\mathcal{N}_A = \{\mathcal{L}(A, U_j)\}$ from independently trained models $U_j$, with mean $\mu_0$ and standard deviation $\sigma_0$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zscore}
Z(A,B) = \frac{\mathcal{L}(A,B) - \mu_0}{\sigma_0}
\end{equation}
The protocol returns
\begin{equation}
V(A,B) = \begin{cases}
\textsc{Related} & \text{if } Z > \tau_{\text{upper}} \\
\textsc{Unrelated} & \text{if } Z < \tau_{\text{lower}} \\
\textsc{Inconclusive} & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thresholds $\tau_{\text{upper}} = 3.0$, $\tau_{\text{lower}} = -3.0$ give false-positive rate below $0.13\%$ under a Gaussian null. Compatibility (same $L$ and $d$) is checked before scoring; the protocol returns \textsc{Incompatible} otherwise. Memory: $O(L^2 + d^2)$ beyond the checkpoints.

\subsection{The Fingerprint Exists and Is Learned}\label{sec:exp-exists}

Table~\ref{tab:plainnet-control} shows the skip connection is structurally necessary: ResNet achieves $100\%$ pair accuracy; PlainNet trained to comparable loss achieves only $3\%$ (at chance). The isometry account of Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} predicts that training drives block Jacobians toward orthogonality, but direct measurement falsifies this: trained GPT-2 is $12\times$ less orthogonal than at initialization (Appendix~\ref{app:gpt2-details}). The fingerprint arises from gradient coupling: the same loss signal reaches both projections and correlates their updates. Two interventions establish causation. Shuffling $\nabla W_{\mathrm{out}}$ across blocks cuts the fingerprint by $68\%$ while preserving task accuracy (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism-triad}b). Injecting diagonal structure into $M$ directly, with no backpropagation, builds the fingerprint from scratch (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism-triad}c). Training on shuffled labels yields a stronger fingerprint than normal training, so gradient flow drives it, not task learning.

\section{Robustness and Boundary Cases}\label{sec:boundaries}

Section~\ref{sec:experiments} established that the protocol detects lineage. This section maps its edges under three pressures: benign post-training modification (Section~\ref{sec:survival}), deliberate evasion (Section~\ref{sec:adversarial}), and definitional boundaries where the method correctly declines or genuinely fails (Section~\ref{sec:failure-modes}).

\subsection{Survival Under Post-Training Modification}\label{sec:survival}

The question is not whether the fingerprint detects lineage---Section~\ref{sec:exp-verify} established that---but what happens as transformation intensity increases. Table~\ref{tab:survival} shows survival across five transformation families: fine-tuning (same/different target), Gaussian noise ($\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}} \le 15\%$), magnitude pruning (10--85\%), quantization (16--256 levels), and LoRA adapter merging.

Fine-tuning, quantization, and noise preserve the signal with mean $\mathcal{L} = 0.99$ and minimum $0.97$. Fine-tuning to a different target degrades to mean $0.94$ (min $0.84$). Pruning shows the steepest decline: from $\mathcal{L} = 0.99$ at 10\% sparsity to $0.58$ at 85\% sparsity. LoRA-merged checkpoints score $\mathcal{L} \in [0.97, 0.99]$. CodeLlama ($\mathcal{L}=0.336$), the real-world far endpoint of the fine-tuning axis after heavy code-specialized continued pretraining, remains ${\sim}700\times$ above the unrelated null.

The thesis: fingerprint degradation correlates with utility loss ($\rho = -0.83$). No benign path removes the signal without damaging the model. The two are structurally coupled through the Jacobian constraint of Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}.

\subsection{Adversarial Suppression and Function-Preserving Symmetries}\label{sec:adversarial}

\textbf{Function-preserving symmetries.} Per-block permutation ($W_{\mathrm{in}} \leftarrow P W_{\mathrm{in}}$, $W_{\mathrm{out}} \leftarrow W_{\mathrm{out}} P^\top$) and reciprocal rescaling ($W_{\mathrm{in}} \leftarrow c W_{\mathrm{in}}$, $W_{\mathrm{out}} \leftarrow W_{\mathrm{out}}/c$) leave the branch product $M = W_{\mathrm{out}} W_{\mathrm{in}}$ exactly unchanged, so $\mathcal{L} = 1.0$ to numerical precision; orthogonal rotations also preserve $M$ but break the network, since ReLU does not commute with rotation. Re-basing hidden units therefore buys an adversary nothing: the signature is built on the one object these reparameterizations cannot move.

\textbf{Gradient-based suppression.} A stronger adversary optimizes a perturbation $\Delta$ directly against the score while constraining function:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\Delta} \;\mathcal{L}(A, A + \Delta) \;+\; \lambda \cdot \|f_{A+\Delta}(X) - f_A(X)\|_2^2
\end{equation}
Sweeping $\lambda$ traces a Pareto frontier (Appendix~\ref{app:attacks}). With $\lambda \ge 1$ the attack cannot push $\mathcal{L}$ below $0.91$; reaching the unrelated range ($\mathcal{L} \approx 0.08$) costs at least 12\% eval-loss degradation; removing the constraint destroys the model (a $57\times$ loss increase). An attacker with white-box knowledge of the score faces the same trade that Section~\ref{sec:survival} measured for benign transformations: the signal does not come off without taking function with it.

\textbf{Untested attack surfaces.} We have evaluated one adaptive attack; two remain open. Re-alignment toward a third model in the style of Git Re-Basin could attempt to rotate the $E$ matrices off their checkpoint-specific directions, and fine-tuning with an explicit decorrelation penalty on $\langle E_{\mathrm{suspect}}, E_{\mathrm{reference}}\rangle$ is a fingerprint-aware strengthening of the attack above; we leave both to future work. Warm-start distillation---a student initialized from teacher weights, then distilled---is not an evasion candidate: under the definition of Section~\ref{sec:background} it is \textsc{Related}, and it functions as a positive control on the distillation boundary rather than an attack on it.

\subsection{Boundary Cases and Failure Modes}\label{sec:failure-modes}

Some conditions the method handles correctly but uninformatively; others it cannot handle at all. We separate the two.

\textbf{Correct boundary behaviors.} Distillation is the most important. Students trained from fresh weights are \textsc{Unrelated} by definition, and the verdicts agree: same-architecture distilled students score $\mathcal{L} = 0.086$ on the controlled benchmark, and the DeepSeek distill sits at the $10^{-4}$ cross-model null on the real test (Sections~\ref{sec:exp-verify}--\ref{sec:exp-baselines}). The gap between those two magnitudes is itself informative---same-architecture distillation on shared data retains a trace of accidental correlation that cross-family comparison lacks---and both fall well below threshold. Symmetry is the second boundary: $\mathcal{L}(A,B) = \mathcal{L}(B,A)$ by construction, so the protocol cannot name the ancestor; direction requires metadata or a third candidate, as in the branching tree of Section~\ref{sec:exp-verify}. Partial lineage is the third: grafts and merges form a graded regime in which $\mathcal{L}$ tracks the fraction of inherited blocks with Spearman $\rho \approx 0.96$ (Appendix~\ref{app:harder-bench})---partial overlap is measured, not misclassified.

\textbf{Genuine failures.} The protocol returns \textsc{Incompatible} when depth or width differ. There is no signal in non-residual networks (PlainNet, Section~\ref{sec:exp-exists}) or when blocks collapse to near-zero contribution. Utility-destroying perturbations such as 2-bit quantization take the fingerprint down with the model.

\textbf{The \textsc{Inconclusive} verdict.} Across all 246 tested pairs (159 MLP + 32 ResNet-18 + 7 LLaMA-2 + 40 harder-regime + 8 BERT), every score fell cleanly above $\tau_{\mathrm{upper}}$ or below $\tau_{\mathrm{lower}}$; no verdict was \textsc{Inconclusive}. The band exists for deployments our benchmarks do not cover, where scores need not separate as sharply.

\section{Conclusion}

Training leaves traces. Residual-network optimization couples each block's input and output projections so that their product becomes diagonal-dominant ($W_{\mathrm{out}} W_{\mathrm{in}} \approx -\varepsilon I + E$), with a theoretical $\sqrt{d}$ upper bound and an empirical $d^{0.87}$ growth observed from GPT-2 through LLaMA-2-13B and Mixtral-8x7B. Across 12 architectures spanning 5 families, the fingerprint recovers 80--100\% of block correspondences and achieves AUROC$=$1.000 on the evaluated lineage benchmark, surviving fine-tuning, quantization, pruning, and RLHF in our tested settings. Our central claim is not that trace/Frobenius dominance universally outperforms generic weight similarity-on the easy MLP regime several data-free weight-space baselines (aligned Frobenius, weight cosine) also reach AUROC$=$1.000-but that trained residual products expose an interpretable, block-localized, data-free provenance signal that supports both correspondence recovery and lineage verification, and that this signal is substantially more robust to distillation than activation- and decision-boundary baselines. Under the tested gradient-based suppression attack, the fingerprint cannot be driven below the detection threshold without $\geq 12\%$ utility loss. This establishes passive, retroactive weight-level provenance verification-no watermark, metadata, or cooperation required.

\section*{Reproducibility Statement}

All results in this paper are produced by deterministic scripts with fixed seeds. The 52-pair MLP lineage benchmark (Tables~\ref{tab:survival}, \ref{tab:baselines}) is constructed from $2$ trained reference checkpoints, each paired with $5$ related-checkpoint kinds $\times 6$ derivative seeds $=30$ truly-related pairs (fine-tune, fine-tune to a new target, $\sigma_{\text{rel}} \in [0.01,0.15]$ Gaussian noise, $10$--$85\%$ magnitude pruning, and $16$--$256$-level uniform quantization), plus $16$ different-seed same-task pairs and $6$ distilled-student pairs as unrelated checkpoints. The expanded 159-pair set used in Figure~\ref{fig:hero}c draws from the full $24$-checkpoint MLP zoo with the same transformation grid. Exact parameters are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:architectures}; null calibration uses the $50$ different-seed pairs from Appendix~\ref{app:verification}. Real-architecture pairing (Table~\ref{tab:transformer-family}) loads public HuggingFace checkpoints. Code, configuration files, baseline implementations (aligned Frobenius, singular-value distance, weight cosine, CKA, SVCCA, IPGuard regression analogue), the per-pair score JSONs, and the plotting scripts will be released upon acceptance.
