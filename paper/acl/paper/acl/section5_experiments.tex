\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

We validate the claims of Sections~\ref{sec:diagonal-dominance}--\ref{sec:verification}: fingerprint existence (Section~\ref{sec:exp-exists}), cross-architecture generalization (Section~\ref{sec:exp-arch}), lineage verification (Section~\ref{sec:exp-verify}), baseline comparisons (Section~\ref{sec:exp-baselines}), and survival under post-training modification (Section~\ref{sec:survival}).

\subsection{The Fingerprint Exists and Is Learned}\label{sec:exp-exists}

Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} predicts that the fingerprint is learned, requires the skip connection, and arises from gradient coupling rather than isometry. We test all three.

We train depth-24 residual MLPs (in\_dim$=$24, hidden$=$64) on CIFAR-10~\citep{krizhevsky2009cifar} for 200 epochs (3 seeds) under seven initialization schemes: orthogonal, Kaiming-normal/uniform, Xavier-normal/uniform, uniform, and Gaussian $\sigma{=}0.02$. Input-output block pairing accuracy starts at chance (${\leq}2.1\%$) and reaches 93--100\% after training for every scheme that converges (Appendix~\ref{app:init-ablation}); the chance-level baseline follows from a null model in which all pairs score uniformly (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). Orthogonal initialization satisfies dynamical isometry exactly at initialization yet yields zero correct pairs, confirming that the fingerprint requires training.

Using the same architecture, we compare ResNet-24 against PlainNet-24, which removes only the skip connection. ResNet-24 reaches 100\% pair accuracy (AUROC 0.98); PlainNet-24, trained to similar loss (1.56 vs.\ 1.35), stays at chance (3\%, AUROC 0.51). Training alone does not produce the fingerprint; the skip connection is required.

Then, we measure the normalized Jacobian deviation $\delta_J^{\mathrm{norm}} = \|J^\top J/\|J\|_F^2 - I/d\|_F$ across the GPT-2 family (small/medium/large/XL), comparing pretrained weights against random initialization. Trained GPT-2 Jacobians are 5--12$\times$ less orthogonal than at initialization (GPT-2-small: 0.297 vs 0.025). The fingerprint grows while orthogonality decays, ruling out isometry (Appendix~\ref{app:gpt2-details}).

To test whether gradient updates are themselves diagonal, we train 48-block residual MLPs (in\_dim$=$128, hidden$=$256, CIFAR-10, 50 epochs, 3 seeds) and measure gradient diagonality $g_{\mathrm{diag}} = |\mathrm{tr}(\nabla W_{\mathrm{out}} \nabla W_{\mathrm{in}})|/\|\cdot\|_F$ alongside the weight score $s(M)$. The gradient product stays flat (${\sim}0.15$), meaning individual updates are not diagonal. Yet the weight score climbs from 0.08 to ${\sim}4.0$ (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism-triad}a). The diagonal structure emerges from many small correlated updates accumulating over training, not from any single diagonal gradient step.

Two interventions isolate gradient coupling as the cause. \emph{Necessity:} shuffling $\nabla W_{\mathrm{out}}$ across blocks before each optimizer step (block $i$'s $W_{\mathrm{out}}$ receives gradients from a random block $j$) breaks only the within-block pairing while preserving valid gradients. This cuts the fingerprint by 68\% ($s$: 3.90 $\to$ 1.24) at comparable accuracy (47\% vs 54\%) (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism-triad}b). \emph{Sufficiency:} injecting synthetic updates $\Delta W_{\mathrm{in}} = -\varepsilon \cdot e_i$, $\Delta W_{\mathrm{out}} = -\varepsilon \cdot e_i^\top$ ($\varepsilon{=}0.01$) directly into weights for 3000 steps, with no loss function or backpropagation, builds the fingerprint from scratch ($s$: 0.08 $\to$ 7.57) (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism-triad}c).

\subsection{From Fingerprints to Lineage Verification}\label{sec:exp-verify}

We evaluate the lineage score $\mathcal{L}$ on depth-24 MLPs (same architecture as Section~\ref{sec:exp-exists}). Related checkpoints are generated via fine-tuning, noise, pruning, and quantization; unrelated checkpoints come from independent training and distillation (159 pairs; see Reproducibility Statement). Checkpoints separate (Figure~\ref{fig:hero}c). In Appendix~\ref{app:verification}, Table~\ref{tab:survival}, we observe fine-tune/quant/noise average $\mathcal{L}{=}0.99$ (min 0.97), pruning averages $\mathcal{L}{=}0.81$ (min 0.58 at 85\% sparsity), while independent/distilled average $\mathcal{L}{=}0.08$. Extending to ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10, related checkpoints score $\mathcal{L}{\geq}0.69$ while independent models score $\mathcal{L}{<}0.01$ (AUROC$=$1.000; Appendix~\ref{app:cifar-resnet}).

\paragraph{Controlled language model benchmark.}
To address the gap between synthetic MLPs and uncontrolled public checkpoints, we train 8 GPT-2-style transformers from scratch on TinyStories~\citep{eldan2023tinystories} with known ancestry. Each root (${\sim}$35M parameters: 6 layers, $d{=}384$, 50K samples, 3 epochs) is allocated to calibration ($n{=}2$), development ($n{=}3$), or test ($n{=}3$) splits. Per root we generate 7 descendants: 1 fine-tuned (1 epoch continued pretraining), 1 LoRA-merged (rank-8), 3 pruned (30/50/70\% sparsity), and 2 quantized (INT8/INT6). We also distill one student per root ($T{=}2.0$) from scratch.

High AUROC on unmodified checkpoints could reflect true lineage detection or mere weight similarity. We disentangle them with \emph{function-preserving laundering}: per-block permutation (P) and reciprocal rescaling (D) that leave the network's input--output map exactly unchanged while transforming the weights. Specifically, P permutes hidden units ($W_{\mathrm{in}} \leftarrow P W_{\mathrm{in}}$, $W_{\mathrm{out}} \leftarrow W_{\mathrm{out}} P^\top$), and D rescales them ($W_{\mathrm{in}} \leftarrow D W_{\mathrm{in}}$, $W_{\mathrm{out}} \leftarrow W_{\mathrm{out}} D^{-1}$). These transformations leave the branch product $M = W_{\mathrm{out}} W_{\mathrm{in}}$ exactly unchanged ($P^\top P = I$, $D^{-1} D = I$), so our signature is invariant by construction. Raw-weight baselines have no such guarantee.

Table~\ref{tab:laundering} shows the results. Our method maintains AUROC$=$1.000 across all variants---the measured signature deviation is $|\Delta\mathcal{L}| \approx 10^{-8}$, confirming algebraic invariance. Raw-weight baselines collapse: aligned Frobenius drops to 0.495 under P alone and to 0.000 under any D; singular-value distance survives P (spectrum is permutation-invariant) but fails D; weight cosine partially survives both but degrades to 0.805 under PD.

A scale-aware Re-Basin baseline~\citep{ainsworth2023git}, which explicitly solves a per-block permutation and scale optimization to undo the laundering, also achieves 1.000 on all variants. The distinction is cost: our signature is invariant with zero alignment work, while Re-Basin must solve $L$ Hungarian assignments plus $L$ least-squares fits for every pair.

We replicate the laundering experiment across four model families to confirm the invariance is not LLaMA-2-specific (Table~\ref{tab:laundering-llm}). On each base$\to$fine-tune pair, P permutes $W_{\mathrm{gate}}$/$W_{\mathrm{up}}$ rows and $W_{\mathrm{down}}$ columns; D$_{\mathrm{m}}$ (mild rescaling, log-uniform $[0.5, 2]$) scales $W_{\mathrm{up}}$ rows against $W_{\mathrm{down}}$ columns. Our score is invariant: $\Delta\mathcal{L} < 10^{-7}$ across all families. Weight cosine collapses under P (0.95$\to$0.003) but survives D$_{\mathrm{m}}$. Unrelated controls (OpenLLaMA for LLaMA-2, Mistral for LLaMA-3) stay at the ${\leq}10^{-4}$ null regardless of laundering, confirming no false positives.

\subsection{Survival Under Post-Training Transforms}\label{sec:survival}

Section~\ref{sec:exp-verify} established that the fingerprint detects lineage; here we measure how it degrades as transformation intensity grows. Appendix~\ref{app:verification}, Table~\ref{tab:survival} covers five transformation families: fine-tuning (same and different target), Gaussian noise ($\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}} \le 15\%$), magnitude pruning (10--85\%), quantization (16--256 levels), and LoRA adapter merging.

Fine-tuning, quantization, and noise leave the signal essentially intact (mean $\mathcal{L} = 0.99$, min $0.97$), and LoRA-merged checkpoints score $\mathcal{L} \in [0.97, 0.99]$. Fine-tuning to a different target degrades the score to a mean of $0.94$ (min $0.84$). Pruning declines most steeply, from $\mathcal{L} = 0.99$ at 10\% sparsity to $0.58$ at 85\%. The real-world extreme of the fine-tuning axis is CodeLlama: after heavy code-specialized continued pretraining it scores $\mathcal{L}=0.336$, yet remains ${\sim}700\times$ above the unrelated null. Across all families, degradation correlates with utility loss ($\rho = -0.83$): no tested transformation removes the signal while preserving the model.

We test adversarial suppression. A white-box adversary optimizes $\min_\Delta \mathcal{L}(A,A+\Delta) + \lambda \cdot \|f_{A+\Delta}(X) - f_A(X)\|^2$, sweeping $\lambda \in \{0, 10^{-2}, 10^{-1}, 1, 10\}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:attacks}). With $\lambda \ge 1$, the attack cannot push $\mathcal{L}$ below $0.91$. Reaching the unrelated range ($\mathcal{L} \approx 0.08$) requires $\lambda = 10^{-2}$ and costs 12\% eval-loss degradation; removing the utility constraint ($\lambda = 0$) destroys the model. The adversary faces the same coupling that makes benign transformations preserve the signal: the fingerprint lives in the correlated component of $W_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $W_{\mathrm{out}}$, which encodes the branch's learned function.
